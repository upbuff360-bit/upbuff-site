## What is conversational BI?

Business intelligence (BI) is the practice of turning business data into information people can act on. Traditionally, that meant reports, dashboards and analysis tools built by specialists.

Conversational BI keeps the same goal but changes how you get there. You talk to your data in plain language, the way you would ask a colleague. The system understands the question, finds the right data, and shows you the result.

Here is a simple example in Clarior:

- **You ask:** "How did our sales perform last month compared with the month before?"
- **Clarior answers:** a short table of both months, the difference, and a chart you can save to a dashboard.

Then you can carry on with a follow-up question, such as "Break that down by region" or "Show only the top 10 customers". The conversation keeps its context, so you don't start from scratch each time.

## How it works, step by step

Most tools follow the same five steps:

1. **You ask a question in everyday language.** No query language, no report menus.
2. **AI interprets the question.** It works out what you mean: which data, which time period, which filters and which measures.
3. **The system prepares a database query.** Using your question and the structure of your database (which tables and fields exist, not your records), it builds a query within the access you are allowed.
4. **The query runs and you see the answer.** The query runs against your business data, and the result comes back as a table or chart. In Clarior, it runs inside your own environment through a read-only gateway, so your records stay with you.
5. **You explore further.** You ask follow-up questions, change the view, or save useful answers to a dashboard.

In other words, the AI handles the translation from human language to data query. As a result, you spend your time on the answer, not on how to get it.

## Conversational BI vs traditional BI

Traditional BI is not going away. Formal reports, statutory reports and carefully designed dashboards still have their place. However, the two approaches suit different jobs.

By traditional BI here, we mean fixed ERP reports and the queue for custom reports, the way most SAP Business One teams work today. Interactive dashboard tools such as Power BI already let trained users filter and drill down. Conversational BI goes a step further: anyone can ask a brand-new question in plain language.

| | Traditional BI | Conversational BI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you get an answer | Open a report or dashboard, set filters | Type a question in plain language |
| Who can use it | People trained on reports and BI tools | Anyone who can describe what they need |
| New questions | Often need a new report built by IT or a consultant | Ask straight away |
| Follow-up questions | Re-run the report with new filters | Ask a follow-up in the same conversation |
| Best for | Fixed, repeated reporting and formal outputs | Everyday questions and quick exploration |

The biggest difference is the report backlog. With traditional BI, every new business question can become a request in someone's queue. With conversational BI, most everyday questions are answered by the person who asked them.

## Conversational BI vs a general AI chatbot

It is easy to confuse conversational BI with a general AI chatbot. They are not the same thing.

A general AI chatbot answers from what it learned during training and from what you paste into it. It does not know your sales figures or your customer balances. If you ask it about your business, it can only guess, or you have to copy your data into it.

Conversational BI, on the other hand, is connected to your own business data. Its answers come from a query against your system, not from the model's general knowledge. A well-designed conversational BI tool also:

- **Respects user permissions,** so people only see the data they are allowed to see.
- **Reads data without changing it,** so asking a question can never alter your records.
- **Limits what leaves your environment,** so your records and query results are not sent to an outside AI service.

These three points matter even more when the data lives in your ERP.

## Why it matters for SAP Business One users

SAP Business One is a strong system of record. It holds accurate data across sales, finance, purchasing and inventory. Yet many teams still struggle to get quick answers from it. We see the same four problems again and again:

- **Decisions on old numbers.** By the time a report is run, exported and shared, stock, receivables and sales have already moved on.
- **A growing report backlog.** Every new question becomes a request to IT or a consultant.
- **Numbers that don't match.** Each team builds its own spreadsheet from its own export, so figures differ between departments.
- **Only experts get answers.** Owners and managers wait for the few people trained on SAP Business One reports.

Conversational BI addresses all four. Managers ask their own questions, answers come straight from the ERP, and everyone works from the same source. One note on matching numbers: live ERP answers remove stale exports and spreadsheet mistakes, but teams still need to agree what each measure means, for example whether "sales" includes credit memos. Sharing saved dashboards keeps everyone on the same definition. ## What to check before choosing a tool for your ERP

Not every tool that "chats with your data" is ready for ERP data. Before you choose one, check these points:

1. **Is it read-only?** Asking a question should never be able to change records in your ERP.
2. **Where does your data go?** Find out which data stays in your environment and which data is sent to cloud AI services.
3. **Does it follow your permissions?** A sales user and a finance manager asking the same question should see different answers if their access is different.
4. **Is it built for your ERP?** A tool that understands SAP Business One's data model will interpret questions more reliably than a generic one.
5. **Can you check the answers?** You should be able to verify results against your standard reports, especially in the first weeks.
6. **Can you save and share results?** Useful answers should be easy to keep in a dashboard, so you don't ask the same question every morning.

## The limits of conversational BI

Conversational BI is powerful, but it is not magic. It is worth being clear about its limits:

- **Clear questions get better answers.** A specific question such as "Sales last month by region" works better than a vague one such as "How are we doing?" Good tools ask for clarification when a question is vague.
- **Answers are only as good as your data.** If items are posted late or master data is messy, the answers will reflect that.
- **It does not replace formal reporting.** Statutory reports, audits and month-end packs still belong in your standard reporting process.
- **Verify early on.** When we test conversational BI with SAP Business One, we check its answers against SAP Business One's standard reports. We recommend you do the same during your first weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does conversational BI mean?**
Conversational BI means using everyday language to ask questions about your business data and getting the answers as tables, charts or dashboards. It combines business intelligence with a conversational, question-and-answer way of working.

**Is conversational BI the same as conversational analytics?**
The two terms are used almost interchangeably. Both describe asking questions of your data in natural language. "Conversational BI" usually puts more weight on business reporting and dashboards, while "conversational analytics" is sometimes used more broadly.

**Do I need to know SQL or reporting tools to use conversational BI?**
No. That is the main point of conversational BI. The AI turns your question into a database query, so you don't need to write one.

**Can conversational BI change data in SAP Business One?**
It should not. For ERP data, choose a read-only tool. Clarior, for example, only runs read-only database queries against [SAP Business One](https://www.sap.com/products/erp/business-one.html).

**Will conversational BI replace our BI dashboards?**
Usually not. Conversational BI is best for everyday questions and quick exploration, while traditional dashboards remain useful for fixed, repeated reporting. Many teams use both, and save their most useful conversational answers as dashboards.
